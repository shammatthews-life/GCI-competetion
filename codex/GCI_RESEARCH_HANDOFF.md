## What the project has learned

### Why v15 works

The strongest recipe is an ensemble that extracts repeated loan-product identity from the application fields and then combines models with different views of the same applicants. The annuity field is approximately generated by a tariff relationship, `AMT_ANNUITY ≈ Q × AMT_CREDIT + F`; for many cash loans, `F` is near 129.5. Fee-corrected Q keys at several precisions, other tariff keys, and leakage-safe product target encodings expose useful repeated-product risk.

The v15 pipeline adds engineered financial and external-score features, cross-fitted residual representations, EXT/LOAN/PERSON views, five LightGBM members per branch, ExtraTrees variants, and product-native CatBoost. Its predictions are combined in rank space. The cached v15 reconstruction gives OOF AUC **0.77404144**, matching the notebook's 0.77404 reference. The reported public score 0.77891 is a separate evaluation and should not be compared numerically to OOF AUC.

The large lesson from v15 and the follow-up work is **incremental error diversity matters more than standalone score**. CatBoost-E1 gained about 0.005 as a standalone model, but its rank correlation with the original CatBoost was 0.9758 and replacing/averaging it into the full ensemble gained only about 0.00000/0.00002. A new branch must fix ordering errors left by all of v15, not merely score well alone.

### Which research signals held up

- **Loan-product decoding:** repeatedly useful in compact models and already central to v15. New product-only target encoding is not an incremental idea because v15 already includes related Q/P product keys and encodings.
- **Smoothed hierarchical product encodings:** Q4 shrunk toward Q3 and contract context produced a large gain over a compact baseline in archived experiments. Product × applicant and product × formula/burden encodings also looked promising in that compact screen, but they did not reproduce reliably in the fresh `v_1` confirmation. Those early deltas are not v15 gains.
- **Household and bureau inquiry specialists:** the matched `claude_7` test found a small positive signal at a fixed 1% blend weight, but only **+0.000017 to +0.000023** OOF AUC. Even the post-hoc 5% grid peaked at +0.000071, below the project's +0.00015 gate and selected on the same folds. Do not submit these blends.
- **Tariff-distance features:** they improved their compact baselines on six archived folds, but v20/v21 public probes did not beat 0.77891 (reported 0.7789, then 0.77889/0.77888). Compact-model gains did not transfer to the champion.
- **Product-conditioned confirmation:** `v_1` rejected product × applicant on both seeds. Product × formula/burden was positive on one seed but negative on every fold of the second. Do not repeat the same blocks unchanged.
- **Bureau bursts, ordinary category pairs, and residual correction:** bureau bursts were neutral/negative in full-pipeline checks; pair target encodings were unstable; the residual probe was below the incumbent and had train/inference iteration mismatch plus the wrong baseline comparison.
- **Weak standalone candidates:** v2 scored 0.77198 publicly; v3 scored 0.77528; neither establishes a v15 improvement. Keep the earlier v15 result as incumbent.

## Important validation and implementation cautions

1. Never promote a feature because it improves a compact model. Compare the complete fixed v15 blend on identical held-out rows.
2. Target encodings must be cross-fitted inside the outer training partition. Outer validation labels must not influence any training-row encoded features used to fit the candidate model.
3. The exploratory v33 script is not a validated experiment: it writes a test blend without OOF scoring. Its feature TE values are made from a 5-fold cross-fit over the full training set; that is not safe for an outer-CV comparison because an outer validation fold's labels can enter encodings attached to other training rows. Do not use its CSV as evidence or repeat that validation pattern.
4. The five v15 folds have already seen extensive model selection. Bootstrap intervals on those same folds are conditional and understate selection uncertainty. Freeze a hypothesis/weight before a fresh confirmation split.
5. Public score changes can be noisy; preserve the v15 CSV and its 0.77891 result as fallback. No local OOF gain guarantees a public leaderboard gain.

## Best next use of the research

The data contain only the application table, so external-history signals cannot be recovered from these files. Product backoff and household affordability have now been added directly to a v15 member and failed matched validation; household and bureau specialists also added almost nothing in an ensemble blend. Avoid another broad feature sweep and do not submit these candidates.

The remaining high-value work is to improve validation/code reliability before spending more model compute. The notebook caches arrays by short filenames but does not store a data/source/config fingerprint or validation-row indices in every cache. A safer optimized copy should add cache manifests, atomic writes, fold-index arrays, and explicit library versions, and should compare the production test blend against a validation procedure that mirrors it. Preserve the original v15 notebook and submission unchanged. Only resume score experiments for a new, domain-grounded hypothesis with a predeclared full-ensemble test.

Given the limited features and the already small incremental effects, an additional large leaderboard gain from a minor feature edit is unlikely. Keep v15 as fallback and avoid treating a small reused-fold OOF fluctuation as evidence of public-score improvement.

### Promotion gate

Before creating any candidate submission, require at least **+0.00015 pooled OOF AUC**, positive deltas on at least **4/5 folds**, and a paired-bootstrap 95% lower bound above zero, followed by a frozen confirmation on a fresh split. Treat this as a research filter, not a promise of leaderboard improvement.
